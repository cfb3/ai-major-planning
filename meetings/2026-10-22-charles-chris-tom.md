## Where the CS program should go

Charles and Chris share this view (still being considered, not decided):

- For about a decade the CS degree has worked as a **service degree** that prepares students for
  software jobs. Only about 5–10% go into research or graduate school.
- Make that explicit with **two degrees**:
  1. **CS / ML (research):** math, machine learning, theory, less programming. Possible tracks:
     ML, LLMs, maybe quantum computing.
  2. **AI Software Engineering (service):** prepares most students for industry software jobs
     done with AI. This would drive most of the enrollment.
- Research faculty might welcome it: the CS degree becomes more research-focused, and they teach
  more advanced courses closer to their research.
- Faculty buy-in is the main obstacle. Chris's framing: the first question isn't what the
  curriculum looks like, it's **how many hours each faculty member will commit** to building it.
  If the total is too low, the effort isn't viable.
- Enrollment is expected to drop further in 2027–28. That makes the direction more urgent.

See [ideas/two-degree-structure.md](../ideas/two-degree-structure.md).
