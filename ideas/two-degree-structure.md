# Two Degrees: Research CS/ML and AI Software Engineering

- **Author:** Charles and Chris (from the 2026-10-22 conversation)
- **Date:** 2026-10-22
- **Type:** program structure
- **Maturity:** sketch

## The idea

Split the CS program into two degrees that match what students actually do:

| | CS / ML (research) | AI Software Engineering (service) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| For | Grad school, research, specialized technical roles | Industry software jobs done with AI |
| Share of students | Small, about 5–10% today (estimate) | Most students |
| Emphasis | Math, machine learning, theory; less programming | Building production software with AI: systems, data, UI/UX, process, testing, DevOps |
| Possible tracks | ML, LLMs, maybe quantum computing | See [curriculum-skeleton.md](curriculum-skeleton.md) |

## Why it matters

- For about a decade the CS degree has worked as a service degree. Naming that makes it possible
  to design for it.
- AI tools now do much of the programming. That changes what each group of students needs.
- Enrollment is expected to drop further in 2027–28 (Charles, 2026-10-22). A clear direction
  matters before then.
- Research faculty may support it: a research-focused CS degree lets them teach more advanced
  courses closer to their research.

- AI Software Engineering grows out of the AI/SWD work in this repo. TCSS 461 (taught as 490

## Open questions / risks

  measuring it in hours per person.

- What stays shared between the two degrees, such as intro courses and core theory?
- Realistic timeline: can anything be ready this academic year?
