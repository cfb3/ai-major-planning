## Update 2026-10-23: a B.A. in Artificial Intelligence

Charles and Chris refined the service side into a **B.A. in Artificial Intelligence**, made by
reshaping the existing B.A. or creating a new one (open); the **B.S. stays the research and grad-school track**, with
possible tracks such as AI/ML, quantum computing, and others. The B.A. is
practice-oriented ("creating with AI") and produces expert AI managers for the AI-capable
workforce, not graduates for traditional
software jobs, which are shrinking (Charles, 2026-10-23). It is portfolio-based, needs no calculus, and requires a minor
from another unit so students apply AI in a domain. Curriculum blocks: programming literacy,
software development with AI, data, networking and security, QA and testing, ethics/tech and
society (with SIAS), a **senior design capstone (2–3 courses)**, one block left open for
colleagues' suggestions, plus the minor. Details:
[meetings/2026-10-23-charles-chris.md](../meetings/2026-10-23-charles-chris.md). The table below
is the 2026-10-22 version; where they differ, this update supersedes it. The deck shows an
illustrative 75/25 service/research split (Charles's choice for the presentation); the 5–10%
research figure below is the earlier estimate.

## The idea

Split the CS program into two degrees that match what students actually do:

| | CS / ML (research) | AI Software Engineering (service) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| For | Grad school, research, specialized technical roles | Industry software jobs done with AI |
| Share of students | Small, about 5–10% today (estimate) | Most students |
| Emphasis | Math, machine learning, theory; less programming | Building production software with AI: systems, data, UI/UX, process, testing, DevOps |
| Possible tracks | ML, LLMs, maybe quantum computing | See [curriculum-skeleton.md](curriculum-skeleton.md) |

## Why it matters

- For about a decade the CS degree has worked as a service degree. Naming that makes it possible
  to design for it.
- AI tools now do much of the programming. That changes what each group of students needs.
- Enrollment is expected to drop further in 2027–28 (Charles, 2026-10-22). A clear direction
  matters before then.
- Research faculty may support it: a research-focused CS degree lets them teach more advanced
  courses closer to their research.
