# ABET and Design Electives for TCSS 461

Researched 2026-10-22 by Claude for Charles. Sources at the bottom.

## Bottom line

1. **TCSS 461 is already an approved design elective.** The B.S. in Computer Science & Systems
   requirements page lists it with the other design electives. No vote to *make* it one should be
   needed. (Confirm with Aubree or an advisor, since the page may not match the latest catalog.)
2. **What ABET actually requires is program-level, not course-level.** ABET doesn't define a
   "design elective." It requires the CS program, as a whole, to include a **major project that
   integrates knowledge and skills from earlier course work**, and to show students reach six
   outcomes. The design-elective requirement is how the CSS program meets that. A team capstone
   that builds on earlier course work and uses several design elements is a good fit.
3. **What 461 would contribute to ABET assessment:** mainly outcomes 2 (design, implement,
   evaluate), 5 (teamwork), 3 (communication) and 6 (CS theory and software fundamentals). Which
   outcomes CSS actually assesses in which courses is in the program's own ABET mapping. Ask
   whoever runs CSS assessment.

## The UW Tacoma requirement

From the B.S. CSS requirements page (accessed 2026-10-22), under Senior Electives: students take
25 elective credits of 300- or 400-level TCSS courses, including 5 credits from approved design
electives:

> TCSS 437, 445, 450, 452, 460, 461, 465, or 491

So 461 already meets the same requirement as 460, which matches the plan to replace 460.

## ABET Computing Accreditation Commission criteria, 2026–2027

**General Criterion 3, student outcomes.** Graduates of the program will be able to:

1. Analyze a complex computing problem and apply principles of computing and other relevant
   disciplines to identify solutions
2. Design, implement, and evaluate a computing-based solution to meet a given set of computing
   requirements in the context of the program's discipline
3. Communicate effectively in a variety of professional contexts
4. Recognize professional responsibilities and make informed judgments in computing practice
   based on legal and ethical principles
5. Function effectively as a member or leader of a team engaged in activities appropriate to the
   program's discipline

**Computer Science program criteria** add:

6. Apply computer science theory and software development fundamentals to produce
   computing-based solutions

They also require, among the curriculum topics, "a major project that requires integration and
application of knowledge and skills acquired in earlier course work." That is the requirement a
capstone-style design elective serves.

(Paraphrased lightly; see the criteria PDF for exact wording.)

## How the 461 capstone lines up

| ABET item | 461 capstone (syllabus draft) |
| --- | --- |
| Major project integrating earlier course work | Team capstone, weeks 4–10; builds on TCSS 360 and the course's weekly topics |
| Outcome 2: design, implement, evaluate | Requirements, architecture, data and UI/UX design, deployed product, test plan and verification report |
| Outcome 5: teamwork | Team-based capstone with individual accountability |
| Outcome 3: communication | Design review, final presentation, retrospective |
| Outcome 6: CS theory and software fundamentals | Architecture and trade-off reasoning, re-engineering, verification |
| Outcome 4: professional responsibility | Week 10 professional practice; security review |

## Also relevant to the AI major

The 2026–2027 criteria document includes **proposed** ABET program criteria for "Artificial
Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Similarly Named Computing Programs" (lead society CSAB). They
add an AI outcome and require at least 42 semester credit hours of AI coursework, a major project,
and at least 9 semester credit hours of math and statistics. They are proposed, not final. Worth
a closer look if an AI-named degree ever seeks ABET accreditation; see
[ideas/two-degree-structure.md](../ideas/two-degree-structure.md). CSAB also reports proposed
General Criteria changes on responsible use of AI in the curriculum, with final action expected
at the Fall 2027 ABET meeting.
